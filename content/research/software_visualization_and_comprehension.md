Software systems are difficult to comprehend when their structure and properties are distributed across large numbers of files, classes, and methods. My work explores how familiar spatial metaphors and interactive environments can make these relationships easier to investigate.

The [CodeMetropolis](https://codemetropolis.github.io/CodeMetropolis/) project applies the city metaphor using Minecraft as a visualization engine. Our initial [[research/bibliography/codemetropolis_code_visualisation_in_minecraft|CodeMetropolis prototype]] transformed source-code metrics into a virtual world of buildings, districts, and gardens. In a complementary publication, we explored [[research/bibliography/codemetropolis_a_minecraft_based_collaboration_tool_for_developers|CodeMetropolis as a collaborative environment]], where developers and other stakeholders could investigate the same software system together. This perspective links visualization with [[research/human_centered_software_engineering|human-centered software engineering]].

A later development, [[research/bibliography/codemetropolis_eclipse_over_the_city_of_source_code|Eclipse over the City of Source Code]], integrated the visualization into the Eclipse IDE so that it could be invoked within the development workflow rather than used only as a standalone tool. We subsequently extended the city metaphor beyond production-code properties in [[research/bibliography/using_the_city_metaphor_for_visualizing_test_related_metrics|our work on visualizing test-related metrics]], connecting visual comprehension to [[research/software_analysis_and_quality_assurance|software analysis and quality assurance]]. The broader relationship between software analysis, visualization, and test-quality management is discussed in [[research/bibliography/utilizing_static_and_dynamic_software_analysis_to_aid_cost_estimation_software_visualization_and_test_quality_management_statikus_s_dinamikus_forr_sk_d_elemz_s_felhaszn_l_sa_k_lts_gbecsl_s_szoftver_vizualiz_ci_s_teszt_min_s_g_menedzsment_sor_n_|my doctoral dissertation]].

The visual modality is not the only possible way to communicate software properties. [[research/spin-off/software_sensing|Software Sensing]] is an exploratory extension of this idea, considering sound and haptic feedback as additional channels for conveying information about code.
