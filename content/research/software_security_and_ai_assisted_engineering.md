Identifying a security problem is only one part of addressing it. Developers must also determine whether a reported issue matters in their project, locate relevant fixes, and assess the reliability of the information used by their tools.

Our work on [[research/bibliography/known_vulnerabilities_of_open_source_projects_where_are_the_fixes_|known vulnerabilities in open-source projects]] investigates the availability of fixes for disclosed vulnerabilities. A related problem arises in static analysis, where a large number of warnings can obscure the issues that deserve attention. In [[research/bibliography/towards_project_aware_actionability_detection_for_coding_rule_violations|our study of project-aware actionability detection]], we explored an active learning approach to distinguishing actionable coding-rule violations from diagnostic noise across different projects. This connects security-oriented engineering with [[research/data_driven_software_engineering|data-driven software engineering]] and [[research/human_centered_software_engineering|human-centered software engineering]].

AI-assisted development introduces additional questions about the trustworthiness of information on which tools depend. Our collaborative work on [[research/bibliography/cve_poisoning_towards_human_guided_and_cost_effective_detection_of_a_novel_ai_data_poisoning_attack|CVE-poisoning]] investigates human-guided, cost-effective detection of an AI data-poisoning attack involving vulnerability information. This shifts attention from using automation to support security work toward examining the security of the information consumed by AI systems themselves.

A separate collaborative chapter on [[research/bibliography/application_of_generative_ai_in_resource_optimization_in_healthcare_systems|generative AI in healthcare resource optimization]] explores AI in a different application domain; it is related through the broader use of AI for decision support rather than through vulnerability research.
