title: Thesis and Internship Supervision
---

I supervise BSc and MSc theses and internships. Prospective students can arrange an initial in-person meeting to discuss their proposed work. The following guidelines describe my expectations for thesis work.

## Topics

All thesis topics I supervise involve software development. Students either develop new software or extend an existing system, through functional or non-functional improvements or fixes. Writing code is a required part of the work.

Topics may be proposed by me, by the student, or in connection with the student's workplace. In each case, the work may be part of an industrial project or scientific research.

Given my areas of expertise and professional experience, I can supervise work involving:

- Python command-line and network applications.
- Java desktop and command-line applications.
- C# desktop (WPF) and command-line applications.

The chosen topic must address a clearly defined, preferably unexplored problem. If similar implementations already exist, the student must evaluate the differences between their own solution and earlier ones.

Students interested in scientific research are encouraged to explore my [[research/index|research areas]].

## Working together

Where appropriate, a topic can be divided into subtopics so that several students can work on it. Everyone must agree to the collaboration. When defining subtopics, I aim to minimize dependencies between students while retaining the benefits of working together, such as examining problems from another student's perspective.

I prefer spoken consultations every one to two weeks whenever possible. I aim to hold joint consultations across topics, since many general principles apply to all of them. There is also time to discuss individual problems and learn from one another's questions and mistakes.

For meeting formats, appointments and the office location, see [[teaching/consultations|Student consultations]].

## Responsibilities and communication

Students are responsible for handling the administrative matters associated with applying and enrolling in courses, and for familiarizing themselves with current university regulations. I provide assistance when needed.

Knowing my own limitations, I leave proofreading and checking the linguistic quality of the thesis to the student.

Spoken consultations are the primary form of communication. Email and written Discord messages are secondary. For thesis-related emails, include the term `szakdolgozat` or `diplomamunka` in the subject. I accept emails only from addresses ending in `@stud.u-szeged.hu` or `@inf.u-szeged.hu`, sent to [geryxyz@inf.u-szeged.hu](mailto:geryxyz@inf.u-szeged.hu).

I expect continuous work throughout the semesters. Send completed sections as early as possible. Reviewing a section typically takes one week, although this depends on several factors. Major changes close to the deadline may not be reviewed before assessment.

I aim to respond as quickly as possible, but supervision is part of my work, so I generally attend to it during working hours, weekdays from 08:00 to 16:00.

## Applying for thesis supervision

Students approaching graduation can contact me with topic proposals in January or September each year. I decide whether to accept the supervision after our discussion, possibly following further consideration. The initial meeting is always in person; see [[teaching/consultations|Student consultations]] to arrange it.

### Topic proposal

For a student-proposed topic, please email a proposal of no more than three pages at least one week before the meeting. It should contain:

- The student's name, university identifier, degree programme and year of study.
- A brief description of the problem in continuous prose.
- A short list of possible solutions and a more detailed description of the chosen approach in continuous prose.
- The intended tools and technologies, including exact names and versions, with references for less familiar items, described in continuous prose.
- A schedule covering the full year of work, broken into one- or two-week intervals, with one or two sentences for each week.

Use the email requirements specified above.
